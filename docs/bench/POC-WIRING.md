Membrane keypad map (owner 2026-10-16: everything on the membrane pad for now; the Blink is deferred): 1 = temp coolant/oil, 2 = fuel level/ethanol, 3 = speedo data face (tap toggles stock/data face, hold 2 s on the data face rotates the order up, a hold on the stock face does nothing; replaces the overlay idea), A = trip key (tap toggles ODO/TRIP, hold 2 s resets the trip only), B = tach rpm/boost. The rest are spare. Ribbon order R1..R4, C1..C4 is the common layout for these pads; confirm with a meter (press a key, find the row/col pair).

## Buzzer: Star Micronics TMX-12S (owner part, marked "TMX-12S 96D")

The TMX series is Star's magnetic transducer line ("low frequency driving type, high sound pressure", [Star Micronics](https://www.star-m.jp/eng/products/transduc/index.html)), so it is a coil, not a piezo disc. Star's spec list rates the TMX-12 versions at 12 to 14 V and about 1,000 Hz ([Star-Asia product list](https://www.starasia.com/Download/Manual/Buzzer/productlist_by_spec_e.pdf)); a TMX-12SC listing gives 1 kHz, 85 dB at 14 V ([datasheets.com](https://www.datasheets.com/star-micronics)). The exact TMX-12S sheet was not found, and "96D" is probably a date code (inference).

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Hub GPIO6 | 1 kohm resistor -> NPN base (2N2222 or S8050) |
| NPN emitter | GND (hub GND = bus GND = bench supply GND) |
| NPN collector | buzzer (-) |
| buzzer (+) | +12 V bench supply |
| 1N4148 diode | across the buzzer, stripe (cathode) to +12 V; it catches the coil's turn-off spike |

Before wiring: measure the coil with a meter (expect tens to a few hundred ohm; current = 12 V / R must stay under about 300 mA for a 2N2222,; small 12 V magnetic buzzers typically draw tens of mA, but measure rather than assume). Owner bench result (2026-10-18): it clicks on 12 V DC, so it has no internal oscillator and needs a tone; `BUZZER_SELF_DRIVE` stays false. Sweep `BUZZER_FREQ_HZ` 800-3000 Hz and keep the loudest. Volume: `BUZZER_VOLUME_PCT` 100 = 50 % square-wave duty, the loudest a coil can be driven at a fixed voltage. Settings: `firmware/hub/alerts_config.h`.

## Park-lamp sense (car; night mode)

The park-lamp feed is 12-14.5 V with spikes, so it never goes straight to a hub pin (3.3 V max). An optocoupler isolates it:

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Park-lamp feed (12 V with park or headlamps on; must NOT change with the dash dimmer) | 2.2 kohm resistor -> PC817 pin 1 (anode) |
| PC817 pin 2 (cathode) | chassis ground |
| 1N4148 diode | across pins 1-2, stripe (cathode) to pin 1 (reverse-voltage protection) |
| PC817 pin 4 (collector) | hub GPIO2, plus 10 kohm to hub 3V3 |
| PC817 pin 3 (emitter) | hub GND |

About 5.7 mA through the LED at 13.8 V. Lamps on = GPIO2 LOW. The hub debounces it for 300 ms (`firmware/hub/park_lamp.h`) and sets 0x6A1 byte 2 bit 0, which every screen and the knob follow. On the bench, the existing switch to GND does the same job.

Finding the wire: with a meter, find a wire that reads battery voltage with the park lamps on and 0 V with them off, then turn the dash dimmer through its range: the voltage must not change. A feed that changes with the dimmer is the panel-lamp circuit, and it can drop below the optocoupler threshold at low dimmer settings. Confirm the wire colour against the 1999 Neon factory wiring diagram before tapping it (not verified here).

## CAN bus

USB-CAN adapter (120R switch ON) -> CAN board #1 (hub, no termination) -> Blink keypad (no termination) -> CAN board #2 (tach, 120R). Power off: about 60 ohm between H and L. If a mid-bus CAN board has a fixed 120R, remove it. Twist H with L and keep stubs short; tie all grounds together (adapter GND, both ESP32s via USB, 12 V supply negative).
